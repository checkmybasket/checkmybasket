# A Secret Santa questionnaire people can answer quickly

A useful Secret Santa questionnaire asks what someone would enjoy, what they already own and which details matter when choosing a gift. Keep it short, make answers optional and leave sensitive information out of anything the group can read. Eight thoughtful questions are more helpful than a long personality quiz.

## Copy these eight questions

1. Which two or three hobbies or interests would you enjoy a gift connected to?
2. What is one small everyday item you would use?
3. Is there a book, game or similar item you would like? Please give the exact title or format if it matters.
4. What do you already own that your Santa might otherwise buy again?
5. Do size, colour, material or compatibility matter for any of your ideas?
6. Are secondhand, homemade or non-physical gifts welcome?
7. What would you prefer your Santa to avoid? You can name suitable alternatives without explaining why.
8. Which three to five ideas fit our agreed total budget, including any delivery allowance?

Introduce the questions with a note that any single suitable idea is enough. A recipient should not feel they are setting an order or asking the buyer to spend the whole allowance.

## Turn an answer into a useful gift hint

“I like books” gives a direction but leaves a lot of guesswork. “I enjoy short science-fiction novels, prefer paperback and already own these three titles” gives the buyer something they can use.

“Coffee” becomes more useful with a brewing method and whether the person wants whole beans, ground coffee or decaf. Our [coffee gift guide](/secret-santa-gifts-for-coffee-lovers) explains why those details matter. “Gaming” needs a platform for an accessory, or a player count for a tabletop game; see the [gaming guide](/secret-santa-gifts-for-gamers).

These are fictional examples, not a requirement to disclose every preference or possession.

## Ask for choices, not personal disclosures

Leave out home addresses, phone numbers, full dates of birth and questions about health or finances. A shared gift list is not the place to collect that information. Someone can say “please choose stationery rather than food” without explaining the reason.

For food, the buyer still needs to check the actual product's ingredient and allergen information. A broad questionnaire answer is not a substitute for checking the exact item.

Make participation optional. If somebody leaves a question blank, use the answers they did provide rather than pushing for an explanation.

## Put the useful answers where the Santa can find them

If your group uses CheckMyBasket, participants can add appropriate ideas to their group wishlist. A [personal wishlist](/wishlists) is a separate option for collecting ideas from different shops. It starts private; enable sharing only when you want the viewing link to be used.

Anyone with an enabled shared link can view and forward it, so keep the list suitable for that audience. The questionnaire here is a copyable template, not an automatic survey feature inside CheckMyBasket.

## A message to send with the questions

> Please answer whichever gift questions you are comfortable with and add a few ideas within our budget. Your Santa will choose one suitable gift. Cheaper options and alternatives are welcome; please leave addresses and private details out of the shared list.
